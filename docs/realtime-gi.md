# Realtime GI

GI and reflection denoising can now be switched between NVIDIA NRD (default) and NVIDIA OptiX AI (HDR) in rendering settings. OptiX uses full resolution inputs and a spatial HDR model; its quality and cost differ from NRD's temporal reconstruction. See [OptiX setup and limitations](optix-denoiser.md). The GPU validation now runs 218 frames, including a distant camera and live denoiser switching.

Select **Global Illumination → Realtime GI (DXR)** in rendering settings. The existing Off and SSGI modes remain available. Ray tracing must be enabled on a DXR Tier 1.1 device; reflections and ray traced shadows can be disabled independently. GI Intensity controls the result and GI Trace Distance bounds secondary visibility work (default 40 world units).

The implementation shares the existing TLAS, geometry uploads, light list, and material bindings with scene reflections. It estimates one diffuse bounce, with one cosine weighted visibility ray and one randomly selected physical light at the secondary hit. Emissive meshes are sampled separately by triangle area, including world scale, distance attenuation, receiver and emitter orientation, texture, and shadow visibility. This avoids relying on random hemisphere hits to discover small emitters. Each frame reads current transforms and material edits. The GI signal stores irradiance; the receiver applies diffuse reflectance divided by pi. PBR environment diffuse is replaced while GI is active so sky lighting is not added twice. Existing specular environment and HDR emission remain.

With NRD enabled, half the primary pixels are traced in an alternating horizontal checkerboard. REBLUR_DIFFUSE reconstructs full resolution using the existing motion, depth, and normal guides; history is limited to 16 frames with a four frame fast history. Frame sequence gaps, camera changes, resize, and GI mode switches restart accumulation. Changes in emission color, intensity, texture resource, emitter transform, or trace distance also restart diffuse history so old light does not persist after edits. Reflection and GI filter descriptors are separate, including when both effects execute in one command list. With NRD disabled or unavailable, full resolution tracing and a 5×5 depth/normal filter provide a spatial fallback; that path has more temporal noise.

This is a bounded real time approximation, not a multibounce path tracer. The shared ray scene currently treats geometry as opaque; cutout and transmitting materials do not receive material-aware ray intersections. Emission is currently two sided, matching the existing two sided acceleration structure. Secondary rays stop at the configured range, so distant occlusion can be missed. Many small emissive meshes or very high contrast textures can require more accumulation. Equal selection of emissive meshes and triangles is unbiased after the area/pdf correction but is less efficient than a power weighted light tree.

Diffuse GI denoising uses a minimum spatial radius of 2 pixels (formerly 1) and a firefly suppression relative scale of 1.5 (formerly 2). The existing compose dispatch also applies a 3×3 binomial filter with depth and normal rejection to remove residual checkerboard grain. Main and fast history remain 16 and 4 frames, respectively, so no additional temporal lag is introduced by increasing the history limit. Ray count, checkerboard layout, bounce count, texture allocation, and dispatch count are unchanged. The small spatial filter trades some fine lighting detail for smoother illumination; geometry weights keep different surfaces separate. Reflection and shadow denoisers retain their previous settings.

Validation: `scripts/validate-gi.ps1` builds and runs an isolated GPU fixture, reads HDR irradiance, checks offscreen red emission, live color edits, a moving occluder, emission shutdown, a blue emission texture, emitter movement beyond trace distance, and switching to SSGI and back with reflections enabled. It logs GI GPU timings and D3D12 errors under `build/gi-tests`, saves comparison images, and restores the user's editor configuration. `-Spatial -SkipBuild` checks the NRD toggle and spatial fallback; `-NoNrdSdk` builds without NRD. Existing material validation covers raster emission/AO, previews, and GPU driven material channels.

The fixture also logs relative temporal RMS over a 9×9 receiver patch during frames 12–23. This interval includes convergence and is not a steady state noise measurement. Before/after output for noise tuning is kept in `build/gi-noise`; the small flat-floor fixture does not measure the exact room in a user screenshot.

Noise tuning comparison on 2026-10-16: relative temporal RMS in this patch decreased from 0.001506 to 0.001002 (about 33.5%). Receiver red irradiance at frame 23 changed from 6.357253 to 6.355517 (about −0.03%). The mean of four GI GPU timing samples changed from about 0.542 to 0.565 ms on the RTX 3050 Laptop GPU at 600×300; the extra neighborhood reads have a small cost even though ray and dispatch counts are unchanged. The final 170-frame dynamic GI validation passed with zero D3D12 errors. The supplied room screenshot has not been reproduced by this fixture.

## Verified on 2026-10-16

- Debug x64 engine build and shader compilation with `USE_NRD=0` and `USE_NRD=1` passed.
- Both 170 frame GI sequences (NRD enabled and disabled) passed, with zero D3D12 errors.
- On the RTX 3050 Laptop GPU at internal resolution 600×300, the four recorded steady state GI pass samples were 0.526–0.577 ms with NRD and 0.351–0.395 ms with the spatial filter. These timings include GI tracing/filtering; they exclude shared TLAS construction and guide generation. This small three-mesh fixture does not establish the cost of a large production scene or full HD rendering.
- After switching from red to green emission, receiver red irradiance fell from 6.357 to 0.240 and green rose from 0.408 to 6.690 within the test interval. Moving an occluder reduced green irradiance to 0.941; stopping emission returned it to 0.410.
- Existing material GPU validation also passed, including raster emission/AO, material previews, and GPU driven GBuffer parity, with zero D3D12 errors. CPU material asset tests and 14 editor configuration checks passed. A build with the NRD SDK entirely absent has not been run; the compiled SDK's disabled-denoiser path and both shader variants were checked.
